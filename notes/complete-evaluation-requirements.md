# What a complete evaluation of Rory's assertions requires

Historical requirements written before the faithful follow-up. See [current executed results and remaining limits](../FOLLOWUP.md); this document is not a claim that those follow-up tests are still unexecuted.

Status: requirements and proposed completion criteria, not executed results. The existing trials are a **diagnostic pilot**. They establish useful behavior and accounting observations, but do not settle the article's central claim: more useful work from a subscription allowance during sustained sessions.

The central missing experiments are faithful related-worker reuse, sufficiently long sessions to encounter compaction, and attributable allowance measurement. These are required primary comparisons, not optional caveats after an overall verdict.

## 1. Test the full policies on sustained work

Compare the native default with **each of Rory's three supplied blocks**, unchanged except for a documented environment adapter. His second block is especially important because it is the one he says he actually used; the long block is a separately identified proposal. Our bounded-Sol variant is not a substitute for either.

Use multiple project workloads selected before outputs are seen. Include orientation across substantial source/docs, an implementation and regression-repair cycle, review, related follow-ups that reuse earlier discoveries, independent assignments that permit parallelism, and a decision requiring earlier constraints. Start with at least three distinct project/workload families, and repeat independent sessions within each. This is a coverage floor, not a statistically justified sample size.

Keep the same task stream, acceptance requirements, model availability, reasoning/speed settings, normal tool access, and resource limits across policy conditions. A default may delegate if its normal instructions permit it. Run in the intended Codex environment; separately replicate the adoption comparison in Chris's actual BB configuration before claiming it transfers there. The earlier isolated CLI configuration excluded user settings and host skills, so it does not establish that transfer.

Faithfulness means exercising and observing the actual instructions: fresh briefs for newly created workers, related-worker follow-ups, batched related questions, escalation after failure, evidence-bearing handoffs, independent reviewers, and disjoint parallel edits where applicable. Do not impose a fresh worker on every follow-up, fixed Sol routing, or a two-claim verification limit in an arm labeled Rory. Do not rewrite a policy mid-run to obtain the desired behavior. If the agent does not follow a rule, that is an observed policy outcome; a subsequently enforced variant is a separate experiment.

Before this comparison, narrow the common adapter's blanket fresh-context requirement to **new workers** and verify reuse in the harness. Test within a parent process and after a parent turn ends/resumes. Require the same child thread ID, unchanged model/effort, and correct use of a fact learned in its previous task without supplying the fact again. The same worker name is insufficient. If process-per-turn `codex exec resume` does not restore workers, use a persistent session transport for all arms. Do not silently replace resumption with a newly briefed worker.

Measure accepted work, total allowance consumption, full-tree token categories, task execution time, failed attempts, repairs, escalations, repeated investigation, handoff omissions, context footprints, and parent/worker compactions. Include all attempts in cost. A failed task is not a cheap success. Compare equal work and, where useful, accepted work under a fixed allowance cap; do not reward a policy for skipping difficult milestones.

## 2. Measure actual subscription consumption

An isolated CODEX_HOME isolates files, not billing. The existing 40 meter observations exposed one weekly bucket and integer values from 1% to 5%. There is no request-attributed subscription ledger or verified maximum reporting delay in our current evidence.

A defensible comparison needs either provider-attributed counters or an account operationally reserved for the experiment during collection. Other devices, hosted agents, automations, and the supervising/grading assistants can share the account. Local process inspection and an apparently unchanged meter cannot rule them out. A coordinated pause on the normal account provides weaker assurance than a dedicated equivalent-plan account with controlled use; disclose the actual arrangement. Do not stop unrelated work or create a paid account implicitly.

Prepare everything before measurement. A **deterministic runner**, with no supervising or grading model calls during collection, runs the scheduled trials and records quota and execution evidence. Analysis and model-based grading occur after the final meter collection. Progress can be written by the runner without invoking an LLM. Keep preparation/evaluation cost separate and visible.

Calibrate first: record an idle interval, a bounded block of known experiment activity, and bounded post-run readings. Record the bucket, window/reset identity, authentication mode, settings, observed meter increments, and update delays. A stable reading is an observation, not proof every delayed charge has arrived. Do not assume a rounding rule or an upper delay bound that the provider has not established.

Use repeated blocks containing one policy each, in counterbalanced order, with matching workloads. Do not combine both policies into one aggregate delta. Choose block sizes to make the desired difference resolvable relative to meter uncertainty. That may require substantial allowance or collection across more than one reset window; pricing-derived credits cannot establish the required consumption in advance.

Set the maximum quota consumption, settling interval, number of blocks, and meaningful effect size before the measured campaign. Preserve and exclude attribution-invalid blocks if there is competing activity, a reset or bucket change, or unsettled/unexplained readings under the declared criteria. Do not keep spending until a favorable separation appears. If exclusivity or resolution cannot be established, the headline subscription question remains unresolved; more ordinary model runs will not fix that measurement problem.

## 3. Observe context and compaction outcomes

The sustained efficiency comparison must give every policy the **same useful work horizon**, long enough to expose natural compaction when it occurs. Measure accepted work before the first compaction, compaction frequency, recovery/re-reading costs, and correctness of subsequent decisions. Do not make one condition do extra work merely so both compact the same number of times. Fewer compactions on the same workload can itself be a result.

Use a separate boundary-focused test for instruction durability. Put the same operating instruction once in chat or in AGENTS.md, then test compliance before and after actual compaction. Include early decisions, a corrected mistake, and later tasks requiring them. Compare a durable TRAPS record with its absence. Score behavior, not the model's claim that it remembered. If the chat-only instruction is copied into a durable note, record that preservation mechanism.

Predeclare an exposure cap, for example 40 meaningful turns or a fixed time/allowance limit, and a boundary target such as two natural parent compactions followed by scored tasks. If the cap is reached without enough compaction, the natural-boundary claim has insufficient exposure. Forced compaction can supply a separately labeled diagnostic, not proof of natural session longevity. Track reused workers' compactions too.

A smaller input footprint alone does not establish less distraction or better reasoning. To attribute later errors to noisy or mistaken history, add a controlled comparison that holds essential facts/task difficulty constant while varying irrelevant or corrected history. A within-model comparison is necessary to separate context length from differences between model families. No finite test proves 'always good' or 'endless sessions.'

Rory's exact historical Langbox/token-ratio observations require his original tasks, versions, settings, and ideally records, plus access to the relevant models. Without them we can test whether the pattern holds now, but cannot claim to reproduce or refute those historical events. His personal trust and account experience are reported experiences; new trials test their generalization. Internal 'attention' and a 'Smart Zone' must be translated into observable correctness/cost outcomes, not inferred from model introspection.

## 5. Make the verdict defensible before collecting more results

Acceptance criteria must correspond to explicit task requirements and useful functional outcomes. The prior rubric sometimes required more detail than the prompt; do not repeat that mismatch. Use independent regression/acceptance checks and blinded review for judgment-heavy work. Separate factual mistakes, missing required coverage, and stylistic preferences. Preserve unsuccessful runs and all repair cost.

Use development runs to fix the harness and estimate variability. Freeze the actual policies before held-out comparisons. Choose repetitions or a sequential stopping rule from the variability and target effect, with a maximum spend. One run per condition cannot estimate variability; a convenient number of repeats does not guarantee an answer. Counterbalance ordering, record cache behavior and version changes, and report uncertainty and project-specific differences. If selecting the best of several policies, account for that selection in confirmation rather than reporting the best observed run as a general win.

The primary practical outcome is **less actual allowance to complete the same independently accepted work**, or more accepted work under the same allowance, with quality and consequential constraint failures preserved. Before execution, define a practical savings threshold, a quality tolerance, and unacceptable failures. Latency and context headroom remain separately reported tradeoffs. Call a small uncertain result inconclusive; 'tie' requires evidence of practical equivalence. A smaller parent context with greater consumption is a context tradeoff, not a subscription-saving win.

## Required inputs and definition of done

Most fixture, protocol, harness, and grading preparation can be done with existing code and public projects. The external requirements are:

- A controlled account-use arrangement or attributable provider telemetry, and a maximum allowance budget for calibration and the campaign.
- A declared target environment and access to every provider/model included in the claims being tested.
- Rory's original suite/settings/records for exact historical replication; otherwise explicitly limit that portion to present-day generalization.

We cannot responsibly quote an exact campaign allowance or calendar duration before meter calibration and variance/exposure checks. Those checks must produce a bounded execution schedule and spend limit before the larger campaign. This is a substantial follow-up, not one extra five-turn run; expensive preparation does not substitute for covering the main claim.

Completion means a claim ledger with an appropriate experiment and evidence for every substantive assertion, replicated faithful long-workflow comparisons, usable allowance attribution for the headline savings claim, and observed compaction exposure for persistence claims. Each entry ends supported, contradicted, mixed, statistically unresolved, or explicitly not reproducible with its missing prerequisite named. An unresolved main claim prevents an overall good/bad verdict; it must not be relegated to a footnote after declaring the evaluation complete.
